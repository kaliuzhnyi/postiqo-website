A car loan estimate, in three steps

  1. Enter the vehicle price, cash down payment and any trade-in value.
  2. Set your annual interest rate, loan term and payment frequency.
  3. Select a tax preset, check the applicable rate, and compare the payment with the total interest.

Look beyond the payment

A longer term usually lowers each payment while increasing the interest paid over the life of the loan. Compare both numbers when exploring a budget or discussing options with a buyer.

The starting values are examples, not a financing offer. Tax presets use general provincial rates. B.C. vehicle rates and Quebec valuation rules can change the amount due.

A few useful answers

How are car payments calculated?

The tool uses equal loan payments at a nominal annual interest rate divided by 12, 26 or 52. The financed amount is vehicle price minus trade-in and cash down, plus estimated sales tax. Interest is charged on that financed amount.

Are bi-weekly and weekly payments accelerated?

No. These are regular payments, using 26 or 52 payments per year. For a custom term that ends between payment dates, the result includes a smaller final payment. A lender's payment dates, compounding and rounding can differ.

Does the calculator include Canadian sales tax?

Yes. Select a province or territory, then adjust the rate if necessary. Presets are starting points for a dealer purchase, not a calculation of every vehicle tax rule. Check the CRA tax rate reference and confirm vehicle-specific taxes with the dealer.

Can I use a trade-in or a zero-interest loan?

Yes. Enter a trade-in value and set the interest rate to 0% if applicable. This estimate assumes a personal trade-in with no outstanding loan and applies tax to the price after that trade-in. It does not model negative equity.

Is the calculator free to use?

Yes. No account or contact details are needed, and the calculator does not send your inputs to a server. You can also use our free VIN decoder to look up vehicle specifications.
